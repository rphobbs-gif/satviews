The yellow dashed line is where the station will go over the next 90 minutes (one trip around the Earth). The darker shading is the night side of the Earth.

See it from Polson

The space station looks like a very bright, steady star gliding across the sky, with no blinking lights. You can see it only at dusk or before dawn, when your sky is dark but the station, high up, is still in sunlight.

Calculating…

How high is "degrees"? 0° is the horizon and 90° is straight overhead, so 45° is halfway up the sky. Your fist held at arm's length covers about 10°. Predictions use the latest published orbit and are most accurate for the next few days; the station sometimes fires its engines, which shifts later times slightly.
